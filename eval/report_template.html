Which streaming model should the dictation app ship?

We ported three streaming speech-to-text models to Swift and Metal and measured them head to head on this MacBook: accuracy on read, meeting and earnings-call speech, how soon each word appears, and whether each keeps up in real time.

Recommendation

Default Nemotron, 160 ms

Words appear about 0.27 s after they are spoken and are final by about 0.55 s. It uses 13% of its real-time budget and 0.2 GB of working memory. It matches NeMo's own accuracy.

Accuracy setting Parakeet, frozen-prefix h4

The most accurate model that keeps up: 25% fewer errors than Nemotron 160 ms on noisy read speech and 17% fewer on meetings, at the cost of about 0.35 s more delay per word.

Not yet Voxtral Mini 4B

The best on clean read speech and earnings calls, but it doesn't keep up: over a long session its step climbs from 83 ms to about 180 ms against an 80 ms budget. Words also appear about 0.7 s late, and it needs about 7.8 GB. Revisit after the speed work.

These picks cover everything except your own dictation recordings, which haven't been recorded yet. The plan treats that set as the one that matters most, so treat these picks as provisional until it's scored.

Against the reference implementations

Each port is compared with its reference on the reference's own utterances. Nemotron and Parakeet match NeMo to within 0.05 points. Nemotron 160 ms reached that only after the end-of-stream fix (D4), which recovers the final word of utterances whose last audio chunk is short.

Other variants, first 100 utterances

sherpa-onnx runs the same Nemotron 160 ms checkpoint through ONNX Runtime on the CPU. Voxtral's 8-bit weights (q8, and q8h with an 8-bit output head) score the same as 16-bit, and q8h's test-clean transcripts are identical to q8's. The 4-bit feed-forward variant (q4ffn) loses 0.12 points on test-other and is no faster, because unpacking 4-bit weights costs more arithmetic than the memory it saves.

Latency

For every correctly recognised word, we measure from the end of that word in the audio (from forced alignment) to the moment it appears in the live transcript (first shown) and the moment it reaches its final form (final). A word counts as final once the next word has started, since a longer subword could still extend it before then. All times are algorithmic: they assume each step's compute is instantaneous, which the speed section shows is small next to these numbers for every model except Voxtral. Scored on LibriSpeech test-other, first 100 utterances.

Lower and further left is better. Each line runs from the median time a word is first shown to the median time it is final.

None of the three decoders ever changes text it has already shown: partial words only grow and complete words never change (0 rewrites). The app can therefore draw text as it arrives, without flicker. At the same 560 ms setting, Parakeet shows words about 0.2 s later than Nemotron does; we have not yet pinned down why.

Speed and resources

Quiet machine, one 33.9 s utterance, three runs after a warm-up. A step is the work done for each new piece of audio; to keep up in real time, a step has to finish before the next piece arrives. The dashed line is that budget.

Bars show the median step as a share of its budget; the black tick marks the 99th percentile. Voxtral takes an 80 ms step every 80 ms of audio, while the other models take a 160 ms step.

Working memory is 0.2 GB for Nemotron and Parakeet and 2.55 GB for Voxtral (mostly its attention caches). On top of that come the memory-mapped weight files: 1.25 GB for each 0.6B model and 5.25 GB for Voxtral q8h.

Neural Engine and CPU alternatives

Nemotron's steady-state step, converted to Core ML and timed on the same quiet machine, takes 21.2 ms on the Neural Engine, 22.5 ms on Core ML's GPU path and 27.4 ms on the CPU, against 22.8 ms for our Metal path. Their outputs agree with our engine to within 7e-3. The Neural Engine is no faster, but it would leave the GPU free, which matters in the contention test below. sherpa-onnx, which runs the same Nemotron checkpoint on 4 CPU threads, needs 0.25 s of compute per second of audio in int8 and 0.96 s in fp32. Our engine needs 0.16 s.

A 30-minute session

Thirty minutes of LibriSpeech test-clean, run back to back as fast as possible, heats the laptop more than live dictation would. Nemotron and Parakeet stay flat. Voxtral's step grows with its position in the stream and settles at about 180 ms, more than twice its budget. Memory stays bounded in the live path, which the app uses: Nemotron's working memory went from 175 MB on a 10 s clip to 239 MB after 5 minutes, and the harness's own records account for part of that. The transcribe command computes features for the whole file up front, which reached 1.3 GB on the 30-minute file. That cost belongs to the CLI, not the engine.

With another GPU job running

With a Voxtral transcription running at the same time, which keeps the GPU saturated, Nemotron 160 ms's median step rises from 23 ms to 80 ms, still half its budget, but its 99th percentile reaches 216–371 ms. Parakeet h4's median rises to 123–136 ms and its 99th percentile to 204–401 ms, so it falls behind whenever the GPU is busy. The app should give its GPU work high priority, or run Nemotron on the Neural Engine, before a GPU-heavy app next to it can make dictation stutter.

Failure notes

Short backchannels vanish

On AMI, every model sometimes returns nothing for Yeah., 'Kay. or Ah.: Nemotron 160 ms on 5 of 150 clips, Voxtral on 10. That matters little for dictation, where people don't dictate backchannels.

Voxtral cuts endings short

On short, disfluent clips, Voxtral stops early: Oh, you wanna have oh yeah, the users became Oh, you want the…. It uses the reference's exact end-of-stream padding, so this is how the model behaves, not a porting error. Extra trailing silence might help, as the D4 fix did for Nemotron; that is untested.

Nemotron can loop

On one earnings-call clip, Nemotron 160 ms repeated AAA AAA AAA AAA where the other two wrote AAA once. It is rare, but visible when it happens; a repeat guard in the decoder would catch it.

Earnings-22 scores are pessimistic

Several reference clips contain neighbouring speech the transcript leaves out (Thank you. Thank you.). All three models transcribe it, so part of every model's 11–13% is the test set's error, not the model's.

Voxtral writes numbers as digits

three, six month became 3, 6, month. For dictation that is arguably better; the normalizer forgives only some of it, so the WER counts it against Voxtral.

Parakeet can drop a whole clip

One Earnings-22 clip (In the context of Sierra Gorda…) came back empty from both Parakeet modes, while the other two models transcribed it almost perfectly.
